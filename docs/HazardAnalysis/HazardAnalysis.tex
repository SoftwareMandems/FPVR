\section{Critical Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stable Internet Connection:} Users have access to a sufficiently stable internet connection for online features, including forms, multiplayer elements, and reviews.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Online features require reliable network connectivity to transmit user input, synchronize multiplayer gameplay, and submit or retrieve reviews. This assumption allows the system to be designed around the availability of network connectivity.

    \item \textbf{Honest User Feedback:} Users provide honest gameplay reviews and feedback, and accurately report their experiences with the game.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Honest feedback helps ensure that reviews and user evaluations accurately represent gameplay experiences, allowing developers to identify issues and measure goals accurately without accounting for fake comments.

    \item \textbf{Available Storage Capacity:} Users have sufficient available storage space on their systems to install the game and store any locally saved gameplay data.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The game requires storage for its installation files, course assets, and locally saved data. Insufficient storage may prevent installation, updates, or the saving of gameplay progress. It may require additional storage after progressing through levels such as save states and leaderboards, this storage should be available for the simulation to use.

    \item \textbf{Basic Gaming Familiarity:} Users have basic familiarity with video games and standard user interface elements, including start screens, settings menus, and navigation controls.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} This familiarity allows users to navigate the application and access gameplay features without requiring extensive introductory instruction, reducing the complexity of the user interface design.

    \item \textbf{Suitable Hardware Performance:} Users have VR equipment that meets the minimum hardware requirements for running the game.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Meeting the minimum hardware requirements helps ensure that the system can maintain acceptable performance, provide a responsive VR experience, and execute the simulation as intended.

    \item \textbf{Accurate Self-Reported Drone Experience:} Users who identify themselves as competent or experienced drone operators accurately represent their level of drone operating experience.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The system may rely on users' self-reported experience to understand their familiarity with drone operation and interpret their training results. Assuming these claims are accurate helps ensure that user classifications and performance evaluations are based on their stated level of experience.

\end{enumerate}

    AI assistance contributed to several sections of the Hazard Analysis document:
    \begin{itemize}

        \item \textbf{Failure Mode and Effect Analysis:} Assistance with brainstorming potential failure scenarios, their effects, and possible mitigation strategies.
        \item \textbf{Safety and Security Requirements:} Assistance with drafting and refining proposed requirements concerning VR safety, controller input validation, hardware initialization, network reliability, authentication, data privacy, and gameplay record integrity.
        \item \textbf{Non-Functional Requirements:} Assistance with expressing proposed performance, responsiveness, compatibility, and recovery targets in measurable terms.

\subsection{Verification of AI-Generated Content}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{How did the team check AI-generated or AI-assisted material before including it in the document?}

    The team treated AI-generated content as a starting point for brainstorming and drafting rather than accepting it without review. Suggestions were evaluated against the FPVR project's objectives, intended functionality, and existing requirements. For example, AI assistance was used to identify potential hazards associated with VR headset tracking loss, invalid controller inputs, and network interruptions. The team considered whether these scenarios were relevant to the system and whether the proposed mitigations addressed the associated risks. AI-assisted wording was also reviewed for clarity, consistency, and relevance before being included in the document.

    \item \textbf{How did the team verify factual claims, technical assertions, requirements, calculations, and references?}

    Technical claims and proposed requirements were reviewed in the context of the FPVR system and the team's understanding of VR development and software engineering. For example, the proposed requirement to maintain at least 30 frames per second (NFR1) and the target of responding to controller inputs within 50 milliseconds (NFR2) were treated as design targets rather than confirmed system capabilities. These targets would need to be validated through performance testing on supported VR hardware. Similarly, proposed security requirements concerning encrypted communication, authentication, and gameplay record integrity were considered in relation to the system's planned networking and data-storage functionality.

    The team also reviewed requirement identifiers, rationales, and risk ratings for consistency and considered whether each proposed mitigation addressed its associated hazard. AI-generated suggestions were not treated as proof that a feature had been implemented or tested. Any factual claims, technical details, calculations, or external references used in the final document must be checked against reliable documentation or independent calculations where applicable. Where implementation details or test results were unavailable, the document distinguished proposed requirements from verified outcomes.

    \item \textbf{Who is responsible for the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and appropriateness of the submitted document?}

    The team is ultimately responsible for the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and appropriateness of the submitted Hazard Analysis, regardless of whether material was written by team members or generated with AI assistance. AI tools can support brainstorming, drafting, and identifying potential omissions, but their suggestions may be inaccurate, incomplete, or unsuitable for the project's scope. Therefore, the team must critically evaluate AI-assisted content and ensure that the final document accurately represents the FPVR system, its hazards, and its proposed mitigations.
\end{enumerate}
