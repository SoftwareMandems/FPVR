\section{Introduction}

This document presents the Hazard Analysis for the VR-Based FPV Drone Operator
Training Game. The purpose of the project is to provide users with a virtual
environment where they can learn and practice FPV drone operation before flying
a real drone. The system is intended to support both new users who are learning basic 
FPV controls and more experienced drone operators who want to practice manoeuvres, test 
their skills, or become familiar with different training scenarios in a low-risk environment.

For this project, a hazard is defined as a system condition, failure, or
interaction that could result in harm or loss. This may include physical injury
to the user, damage to equipment, loss of data, system failure, or incorrect
training caused by unrealistic simulator behaviour. Although the system is 
primarily a software simulation, hazards may still arise from the use of VR 
hardware, software failures, inaccurate drone physics, or incorrect user 
interaction with the system.

\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis}

The purpose of this Hazard Analysis is to identify potential failure modes of
the FPV drone training system, understand their possible effects, and determine
ways to reduce or prevent these risks.

The analysis considers hazards related to:

\begin{itemize}
    \item VR headset operation
    \item interaction with the user's physical surroundings
    \item drone physics and flight simulation
    \item user controls and input devices
    \item visual and audio feedback
    \item training and scoring features
    \item application performance
    \item misuse of the training system 
    \item security and integrity of the application
\end{itemize}

Possible losses caused by these hazards include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item physical injury from colliding with furniture, walls, or other people
    while using the VR headset
    \item nausea, dizziness, eye strain, or other discomfort caused by VR use
    \item damage to the VR headset, controllers, computer, or nearby property
    \item loss of user progress or settings
    \item application crashes or interruptions during training
    \item incorrect or unrealistic drone behaviour that could teach the user
    improper flying techniques
    \item an incorrect understanding of how a real FPV drone behaves
    \item users becoming overconfident in their real-world flying ability based
    only on their simulator performance
\end{itemize}

This analysis focuses on hazards related to the simulator itself and its use.
The simulator is intended to be a training tool and does not guarantee safe
operation of a real FPV drone or reproduce every condition that may occur
during real-world flight.

\section{Critical Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stable Internet Connection:} Users have access to a sufficiently stable internet connection for online features, including forms, multiplayer elements, and reviews.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Online features require reliable network connectivity to transmit user input, synchronize multiplayer gameplay, and submit or retrieve reviews. This assumption allows the system to be designed around the availability of network connectivity.

    \item \textbf{Honest User Feedback:} Users provide honest gameplay reviews and feedback, and accurately report their experiences with the game.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Honest feedback helps ensure that reviews and user evaluations accurately represent gameplay experiences, allowing developers to identify issues and measure goals accurately without accounting for fake comments.

    \item \textbf{Available Storage Capacity:} Users have sufficient available storage space on their systems to install the game and store any locally saved gameplay data.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The game requires storage for its installation files, course assets, and locally saved data. Insufficient storage may prevent installation, updates, or the saving of gameplay progress. It may require additional storage after progressing through levels such as save states and leaderboards, this storage should be available for the simulation to use.

    \item \textbf{Basic Gaming Familiarity:} Users have basic familiarity with video games and standard user interface elements, including start screens, settings menus, and navigation controls.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} This familiarity allows users to navigate the application and access gameplay features without requiring extensive introductory instruction, reducing the complexity of the user interface design.

    \item \textbf{Suitable Hardware Performance:} Users have VR equipment that meets the minimum hardware requirements for running the game.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Meeting the minimum hardware requirements helps ensure that the system can maintain acceptable performance, provide a responsive VR experience, and execute the simulation as intended.

    \item \textbf{Accurate Self-Reported Drone Experience:} Users who identify themselves as competent or experienced drone operators accurately represent their level of drone operating experience.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The system may rely on users' self-reported experience to understand their familiarity with drone operation and interpret their training results. Assuming these claims are accurate helps ensure that user classifications and performance evaluations are based on their stated level of experience.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Functional Requirements}

\noindent
\textbf{SR1:} The system shall provide users with a safety notice before their first VR session, reminding them to clear their physical play area of obstacles and remain aware of their surroundings.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Users may collide with nearby objects while immersed in the virtual environment, potentially causing injury or damaging equipment.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR2:} The system shall allow users to pause or exit gameplay at any time during a VR session.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Users may experience motion sickness, dizziness, or discomfort that requires them to stop playing immediately.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR3:} The system shall suspend active gameplay when the VR platform reports a headset disconnection or loss of tracking that prevents reliable interaction.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Continuing gameplay when tracking is unreliable may cause unexpected interactions and increase user disorientation.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR4:} The system shall verify that required VR hardware is detected and initialized before allowing users to begin a flight session.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Missing or improperly initialized hardware may prevent reliable controller input and compromise gameplay.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR5:} The system shall validate controller input values before applying them to the drone simulation and reject values outside supported input ranges.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Invalid controller inputs may produce unintended drone movements or unpredictable simulation behaviour.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR6:} The system shall verify connectivity to required external services before enabling features that depend on them.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Service connectivity failures may prevent multiplayer sessions or cause dependent features to behave incorrectly.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR9:} The system shall validate data received from multiplayer clients before accepting it for gameplay state updates or performance record storage.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Malformed or manipulated network messages may cause inconsistent multiplayer behaviour or compromise recorded statistics.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR11:} The system shall notify users when a network interruption prevents access to multiplayer functionality.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Users may otherwise be unaware that multiplayer functionality is unavailable or their session has been interrupted.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Low\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR12:} The system shall disclose what gameplay data is collected, how it is used, and whether it is transmitted to external services before collecting user-associated data.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Users need to understand how their gameplay information is handled to make informed decisions about their privacy.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR13:} The system shall require users to authenticate before accessing personal accounts or user-specific gameplay records, if account functionality is implemented.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Authentication helps prevent unauthorized users from accessing personal information and saved progress.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR14:} The system shall enforce authorization checks before allowing users to view, modify, or delete stored gameplay records.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Authorization prevents users from accessing or modifying records belonging to other users.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR16:} The system shall validate and sanitize user-provided data before processing or storing it.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Improperly handled input may enable injection attacks, corrupt stored records, or cause unexpected system behaviour.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR18:} The system shall terminate or invalidate multiplayer session credentials when a user leaves a session or the session expires.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Invalidating expired session credentials reduces the risk of unauthorized reuse or continued access to multiplayer sessions.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium


\noindent
\textbf{SR7:} The system shall use encrypted communication when transmitting user data or multiplayer session information over a network.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Unencrypted network traffic may expose user information or session data to unauthorized parties.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{SR8:} The system shall restrict access to stored user-specific gameplay records to the relevant user and authorized system components.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Unauthorized access may expose private information or allow gameplay records to be modified.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]

\textbf{Rationale:} Security logs support the investigation of suspicious activity while minimizing the exposure of sensitive data.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}
\noindent
\textbf{NFR1:} The system shall maintain a frame rate of at least 30 frames per second during standard single-player gameplay on the minimum supported VR hardware.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Maintaining a consistent frame rate helps reduce visual discomfort and supports an immersive VR experience.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]

\noindent
\textbf{NFR2:} The system shall respond to valid controller inputs within 50 milliseconds under normal operating conditions.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Low input latency supports precise drone control and provides a more realistic flight simulation experience.\\
\textbf{Risk:} High\\[1em]


\noindent
\textbf{NFR3:} The system shall operate correctly on all VR headsets, controllers, and operating system configurations explicitly listed as supported in the system documentation.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Defining supported configurations ensures consistent operation and reduces hardware compatibility issues.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium\\[1em]


\noindent
\textbf{NFR4:} The system shall restore access to the main menu within 10 seconds of recovering from a recoverable VR tracking or network interruption, provided that the required hardware and services are available.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Prompt recovery minimizes disruption and reduces the need to restart the application.\\
\textbf{Risk:} Medium
