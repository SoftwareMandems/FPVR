\section{Introduction}

FPVR is a virtual reality (VR) first-person-view (FPV) drone training
simulator. It provides single-player free flight and obstacle courses through
a Meta Quest headset, using standalone or personal-computer VR (PCVR)
execution. The software does not operate a physical drone. Its principal
physical risks arise from immersive VR use; misleading training feedback,
unavailable controls, corrupted results, and disclosure of flight data can
also cause harm.

This report applies a preliminary Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA)
to the requirements baseline in the Software Requirements Specification
(SRS)~\cite{srs}. The analysis identifies unsafe conditions, possible losses,
proposed controls, and evidence needed to evaluate those controls. It does
not report completed user tests, measured failure frequencies, approved
risk acceptance, or certification of safety.

The report follows the repository's capstone Hazard Analysis template. The
existing section structure is retained, with a glossary, context figure,
classification scheme, action register, and traceability tables added for
clarity. The repository HA and SRS checklists and the supplied course rubric
guide coverage~\cite{haChecklist,srsChecklist,rubric}. Section~\ref{sec:scope}
defines losses; Section~\ref{sec:boundary} identifies the boundary;
Section~\ref{sec:premises} records operating premises;
Section~\ref{sec:fmea} develops failure cases and actions;
Section~\ref{sec:safety} traces proposed requirements; and
Section~\ref{sec:roadmap} describes review and implementation gates.

\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis}\label{sec:scope}

The analysis covers installation, launch, initial instructions, input and
tracking, simulated flight, course progression, crashes and recovery,
timing and scoring, current-attempt review, pause and exit, and foreseeable
interruption or misuse. Optional AI is analysed conditionally because it
could introduce new failure and disclosure paths. Both seated and standing
operation are required; physical walking is unnecessary. Beginners,
recreational users, students, experienced pilots, parents or guardians,
bystanders, and the project team may be affected.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Losses considered by the analysis}
\label{tab:losses}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.8cm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Potential loss}\\
\midrule
L1 & Injury, fall, collision, nausea, visual discomfort, or distress to a
user or nearby person during immersive use.\\
L2 & Damage to the headset, controllers, computer, furniture, or other
property after physical contact, a fall, or unsafe handling.\\
L3 & Misleading training, unjustified confidence in real-world proficiency,
frustration, or loss of trust caused by incorrect physics, results, or feedback.\\
L4 & Loss of privacy or control of information through unintended retention,
export, external-service transmission, or malicious modification.\\
L5 & Loss of an attempt, unavailable training, avoidable installation effort,
or rework caused by failed deployment, state corruption, or unavailable services.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The intended benefit is safe introductory practice, not a guarantee that
simulated performance transfers to physical flight. Physical-drone control,
pilot certification, multiplayer, and desktop-only play are excluded.
Dedicated FPV transmitters and historical-session review are not confirmed
core features. Multiple environments and immediate access to all levels do
not remove the need for usable instructions and recovery controls.

The purpose is to reduce exposure to foreseeable harm and identify
verifiable requirements before implementation decisions become difficult
to change. The analysis also supports society's interest in responsible
training, accessible interaction, age-appropriate VR use, and truthful
claims. The headset manufacturer remains the authority on device operating
and regional age restrictions; this report does not substitute for that
guidance~\cite{metaSafety,metaAge}.

\section{System Boundaries and Components}\label{sec:boundary}

The controlled software boundary contains the FPVR application, its
configuration and course assets, and application-managed transient attempt
data. Unity and its selected extended reality (XR)/input packages are execution dependencies.
Headset displays, tracking sensors, controllers, the optional PC and link,
operating systems, and the XR runtime lie outside the application's control
but are included in the analysis as potential contributors. Platform boundary
and pause facilities cannot be assumed to prevent every physical collision.
Meta explicitly advises maintaining a clear activity space and recognises
that boundary features may miss people or pets~\cite{metaSafety}.

\section{Failure Mode and Effect Analysis}\label{sec:fmea}

\subsection{Method and preliminary classification}

For each element in Table~\ref{tab:elements}, the analysis considers missing,
incorrect, late, stale or malicious inputs; missing or incorrect outputs;
and interruption during normal and recovery operation. Each failure is
connected to a hazardous state and a possible loss, then to an avoidance,
containment, recovery, warning, or procedural action. This is a requirements
analysis, not a completed reliability assessment. The bottom-up structure
is consistent with FMEA practice~\cite{nasaFmea}; it is not a claim of
compliance with a safety standard.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{Proposed qualitative classification scheme; all assignments preliminary}
\label{tab:classification}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{1.4cm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Label} & \textbf{Meaning and decision rule}\\
\midrule
S1 & Potential serious physical injury to a user or bystander, such as a
fall or impact. Exposure conditions determine the eventual severity.\\
S2 & Potential reversible discomfort, distress, property damage, privacy
loss or material interruption; actual consequence may vary.\\
S3 & Predominantly incorrect training feedback, lost attempt data,
frustration or limited availability without an identified direct injury path.\\
U & Likelihood unknown: no frequency, probability, exposure measurement,
or detection-coverage evidence is available. Applies to every row.\\
P1 & Proposed priority: establish controls and review evidence before
exposing users to active-flight evaluation or releasing a data-transfer feature.\\
P2 & Proposed priority: resolve before acceptance of the affected core
functionality; conditional AI actions apply only if AI enters scope.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The severity label describes a plausible consequence, not its measured
frequency. P1/P2 are proposed action priorities, not an accepted risk matrix.
No numeric risk-priority number is calculated because occurrence and
detection estimates would be invented. A lower-priority item is not
declared safe. Every residual-risk disposition is pending implementation,
verification and team review; the responsible team must record its
decision and evidence before closing an action.

OpenXR distinguishes visible and focused session states, and tracked pose
status from merely valid pose data~\cite{openxr}. Consequently SR-02 must
be evaluated against the selected runtime's actual status reporting; a
last-known pose alone is not evidence of current tracking. The application
must not prevent platform-managed head rendering or assume that it can
recover a powered-off headset. Exact interruption detection and response
latencies are pending platform integration review.

Meta's platform guidance also calls for single-player pause when the
headset is removed or the system menu opens, while focus-aware rendering
continues under the runtime~\cite{metaVrc}. This informs the interruption
proposal and must be checked on the selected release platform.

Safety requirements also depend on the confirmed controller, flight,
checkpoint, timing and review requirements identified in the action
register. Verification must exercise their interactions: for example,
tracking loss while a crash menu is visible must not create a new crash,
restart a timer or advance a checkpoint. Invalid or unavailable AI output
must leave the numerical score and ordinary results unchanged.

Meta safety and age guidance inform operation and instructions, not an
assertion that FPVR is certified or suitable for every user~\cite{metaSafety,metaAge}.
Unity/XR package selection, PCVR platform requirements and distribution
conditions must be checked when versions and supported configurations
are chosen. If accounts, persistent data or external AI are approved
later, privacy, regional age/data obligations and applicable service
terms require a separate review before transfer is enabled. No current
legal-compliance or accessibility certification is claimed.

\section{Roadmap}\label{sec:roadmap}

This roadmap concerns completion and review of the two requirements
documents and the safety gates they establish. It is not a replacement
for the whole project schedule. October 19, 2026 is the draft date;
the dates below are proposed document-review targets, not course deadlines
or completed team activities. No implementation date is invented.

SR-01--SR-07 are proposed for the core safety baseline because they address
risks in confirmed functionality. SR-08 is conditional on AI inclusion.
Additional headset/controller support, FPV transmitters, stored histories,
external services, wind and a more accurate physical model require a
change-impact review; multiplayer or physical-drone control would require
a substantially revised boundary and hazard analysis. Deferred comfort or
accessibility enhancements must not be described as already evaluated.

Closing an action requires an approved requirement or documented
alternative, a reproducible test/review record, its configuration and result,
and an explicit team decision about remaining exposure. The draft's
classifications must be revisited after integration and any significant
scope or platform change. There is currently no accepted residual-risk
record and no completed user-safety evaluation.
