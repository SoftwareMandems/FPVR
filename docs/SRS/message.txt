Transcription notes:
  - The PDFs are "Microsoft Print to PDF" exports of Avenue to Learn rubrics with
    no text layer; text was transcribed from high-resolution page renders.
  - Wording, punctuation, capitalization and the rubric authors' own typos are
    kept exactly as printed (e.g. "Conventiosn", "refernece", "adquate",
    "justifcation", "temot", "Inappropriatel y", "teams SRS"). Only line-wrap
    hyphenation (e.g. "re-quirements") has been joined back into whole words.
  - Criterion names were printed vertically one or two letters per line in the
    PDFs; they are reconstructed here.
  - Each criterion lists every level: Exceeds/Level 4, Meets/Level 3,
    Marginal/Level 2, Below/Level 1.

SRS-1. Document Content and quality                                         / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Document contains all required elements or their equivalent (scope, context
  diagram showing boundaries, symbolic constants (as appropriate), behaviour
  overview, required behaviour, rationale where necessary, nonfunctional
  requirements including performance requirements, normal operation, undesired
  event handling, requirements that are likely/unlikely to change, references,
  stakeholders identified, user characteristics); includes reflections. All
  elements are of high quality.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Document contains most of the required elements listed in the Exceeds
  category.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Document missing many of the required elements or many elements are clearly
  deficient without reasonable explanations for the deficiencies.
BELOW (0.0):
  Majority of required elements are missing or obviously deficient.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SRS-2. Document Organization                                                / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Identifies template; Follows template; if there are changes they are clearly
  documented. Document is written so that: 1. Terms and acronyms are always
  defined before first use and there is a list providing full expansion of all
  acronyms with links to where they are defined in the text.  2. Information is
  defined in only one place (i.e. no redundant information). 3. Each requirement
  has a unique identifier associated with it to facilitate traceability.
  4. Figures and tables are clearly readable and of sufficient resolution.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Identifies template; Mostly follows it, but there are undocumented changes.
  Document is written to follow the 4 items under Exceeds most of the time.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Does not identify template; makes unexplained changes from existing
  templates. Document often misses the 4 items listed under Exceeds.
BELOW (0.0):
  Does not identify template; major unexplained changes from existing
  templates. Document fails to achieve almost all of the 4 items listed under
  Exceeds.

SRS-3. Formatting and style                                                 / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Document is well formatted with: 1. clear headings. 2. appropriate use of
  formatting (i.e., italics, captions etc.). 3. correct grammar and spelling.
  4. fully hyperlinked references to facilitate document navigation.
  5. document perfectly targeted at an appropriate audience of team engineers
  and other stakeholders and could be used to get new team members quickly up
  to speed.  Provides pointers to key preferences where applicable;
  Professional quality document.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Document is generally well-formatted to follow the items listed under
  Exceeds; Near professional quality. Good student quality.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Document has a number of formatting issues with the items listed under
  Exceeds mostly not being achieved.  Clearly a student document produced in a
  rush.
BELOW (0.0):
  Document has major formatting issues and fails to achieve almost all of the
  items listed under Exceeds.  Clearly a student document with little effort
  invested.

SRS-4. Notations and Conventions                                            / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  All notations and terms are precisely defined.  All figures have legends as
  needed and/or notation section clearly define all graphical notations used.
  For instance, the meaning of every arrow in a diagram is made clear.
  Conventiosn used in the document are described and followed consistently.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):

MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Some of the points listed under Exceeds are achieved.  For instance, some
  notations are precisely defined.  No effort to use appropriate notation.
BELOW (0.0):

  little or no effort to define notations and terms are incorrectly defined.

SRS-5. What not How (Abstract)                                              / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Requirements are a "black box" and are free from design decisions.  If design
  decisions are externally imposed, they are documented as constraints, not
  requirements.  The rational for constraints is given and makes sense in the
  context of the project.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Requirements are mostly free from design choices.  If design decisions are
  externally imposed, they are usually documented as constraints, usually
  accompanied by a rationale.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Requirements have design choices without appropriate rational for these
  design decisions.
BELOW (0.0):
  Requirements are often more of a design (the how rather than the what).

SRS-6. Complete, Correct and Unambiguous                                    / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Requirements are complete and correct.  Wording is clear and precise.  All
  requirements are unambiguous and provide domain coverage (say what needs to
  be done for all possible inputs).  The TA can brainstorm a handful of obvious
  requirements and find them all in the document.  A sample requirement to look
  for is a nonfunctional requirement related to installability.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Requirements are mostly complete and correct.  The wording is usually clear
  and precise.  Most requirements are unambiguous and provide domain coverage
  (say what needs to be done for all possible inputs).  The TA can find most of
  their brainstormed requirements.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Requirements are incomplete and/or contain numerous errors.   correct. The
  wording is often confusing, ambiguous and imprecise.  Most requirements are
  ambiguous and do not provide domain coverage. The TA has trouble finding
  their brainstormed requirements.
  [Transcriber note: the gap before "correct." is present in the source.]
BELOW (0.0):
  Requirements are obviously incomplete, with little evidence of effort to
  capture them.  Wording is confused, ambiguous and imprecise.  Requirements
  contain obvious ambiguities and do not provide domain coverage.  The TA
  cannot find their brainstormed requirements.

SRS-7. Traceable Requirements                                               / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Requirements numbered and/or labelled; bi-directional navigation through the
  document is easy. (That is, if A depends on B, then the documentation of both
  A and B will show the dependency information.  For instance, if a
  nonfunctional safety requirement is satisfied by a functional requirement,
  the traceability information appears with both the functional and
  nonfunctional requirements.)  A diagram or table is provided to show the
  traceability.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Requirements are numbered and/or labelled.  Navigation is mostly easy, with
  bi-directional information usually provided.  An incomplete diagram or table
  for the traceability.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Requirements are numbered and/or labelled, but navigation is not easy.  The
  traceability information is often missing, and rarely bidirectional.  No
  diagram or table showing traceability.
BELOW (0.0):
  Navigation is very difficult.  Traceability information is often lacking.
  No diagram or table to show traceability.

SRS-8. Verifiable Requirements                                              / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  All requirements are clearly verifiable; that is, there is some way to
  measure/test that the requirement has been satisfied.  The system shall be
  user friendly is not verifiable.  The device will never break is not
  verifiable.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Most requirements are verifiable.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Some requirements are verifiable.
BELOW (0.0):
  A few requirements are verifiable, but most are not.

SRS-9. Rationale                                                            / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  Detailed rationale provided for all requirements that are not obvious.
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  Rationale provided for the majority of requirements that need it.
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  Rationale missing on many important, non-obvious requirements.  Lacks
  appropriate justification of performance requirements.
BELOW (0.0):
  No rationale provided for the majority of requirements.  No justifcation of
  specifics used in particular requirements.  For instance, "The system must

  comes from.

SRS-10. Focus on Users (Part of Enterprise Design Thinking)                 / 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXCEEDS (2.1-3.0):
  User characteristics are clearly documented; All stakeholders identified and
  documented; The requirements enable the users; The requirements show
  specific and differentiable value (compared to other available options). The
  thoughts, needs and feelings of the users were obviously taken into account
  when drafting the requirements
MEETS (1.1-2.0):
  User characteristics are mostly documented; Most stakeholders are identified
  and documented; The requirements mostly enable the users; The requirements
  mostly show specific and differentiable value (compared to other available
  options). The thoughts, needs and feelings of the users were mostly taken
  into account when drafting the requirements
MARGINAL (0.1-1.0):
  User characteristics are poorly documented; Important stakeholders aren't
  identified and documented; The requirements rarely enable the users; The
  requirements do not show specific and differentiable value (compared to
  other available options). The thoughts, needs and feelings of the users are
  not taken into account when drafting the requirements.  Seems like team did
  not consult any potential users.
BELOW (0.0):
  User characteristics are not identified; stakeholders are not identified;
  Requirements do not enable users; Requirements show no differentiable value.
  Feels like the users were not even considered when drafting the
  requirements.

SRS-12. Basis for Design                                                    / 3

  produced.
BELOW (0.0):

SRS document must:
  [ ] Include: scope; context diagram showing boundaries; symbolic constants
      (as appropriate); behaviour overview; required behaviour; rationale where
      necessary; nonfunctional requirements incl. performance requirements;
      normal operation; undesired event handling; likely/unlikely-to-change
      requirements; references; stakeholders; user characteristics; reflection
  [ ] Identify and follow the template; document any changes to it
  [ ] Define terms/acronyms before first use; acronym list with links to
      definitions in the text
  [ ] Define information in only one place (no redundancy)
  [ ] Give every requirement a unique identifier
  [ ] Readable, sufficient-resolution figures and tables
  [ ] Clear headings, appropriate formatting (italics, captions), correct
      grammar/spelling, fully hyperlinked references; targeted at team
      engineers/stakeholders; pointers to key "preferences" (sic, likely
      "references"); professional quality
  [ ] Precisely define all notations/terms; figure legends or notation section
      (meaning of every arrow); conventions described and followed consistently
  [ ] Requirements are black-box (what, not how); externally imposed design
      decisions documented as constraints with rationale
  [ ] Complete, correct, clear, precise, unambiguous; domain coverage for all
      possible inputs; include an installability NFR
  [ ] Bi-directional traceability (both sides show dependency, e.g. NFR <-> FR)
      plus a traceability diagram or table
  [ ] Every requirement verifiable/measurable
  [ ] Detailed rationale for all non-obvious requirements (justify specific
      numbers)
  [ ] User characteristics, all stakeholders, requirements enable users,
      specific/differentiable value vs. alternatives, users' thoughts/needs/
      feelings considered (consult potential users)
  [ ] Priority requirements identified; phase-in plan/timeline WITH dates
  [ ] Sufficient for an outsider to design, build and test; likely and unlikely
      changes documented with justification; inputs/outputs obvious
  [ ] Reflection answering capTemplate docs/SRS_Reflection.tex questions
  [ ] Gen AI disclosure: tools + versions, how/where used, verification steps
  [ ] Formalize a critical portion with correct, error-free math notation
      (discrete math, FSMs, Z, etc.)                           [LO_SpecMath]
  [ ] Clear system/subsystem boundaries; all necessary assumptions stated
      explicitly                                               [LO_Assumptions]
  [ ] Incorporate external info (supervisor, stakeholders, users, literature,
      web); complete, consistently formatted, cited references [LO_ExtKnowledge]
  [ ] Society as a stakeholder where appropriate; health, safety, cultural and
      other relevant factors documented                        [LO_Impact]
  [ ] All appropriate standards, codes, legal and regulatory factors
      documented                                               [LO_Standards]

Hazard Analysis document must:
  [ ] Title page incl. names; ToC; List of Figures; List of Tables; numbered
      pages; captioned figures; headed tables; revision history; supporting
      material/appendices; introduction (scope, purpose, background, roadmap)
  [ ] Good paragraph structure, concise ideas, flow, navigation references,
      logical subsections; minimal spelling/grammar errors
  [ ] System boundary, scope of HA, definition of hazard, critical assumptions
  [ ] Proper application of an appropriate hazard analysis technique
  [ ] Complete hazard identification
  [ ] Appropriate categorization + elimination/mitigation strategy leading to
      safety requirements
  [ ] Reflection per capTemplate docs/HazardAnalysis/HazardAnalysis.tex
  [ ] Gen AI disclosure (as for SRS)

Process / team activities:
  [ ] >5 high-quality (non-typo/formatting) GitHub issues critiquing the
      assigned team's SRS and HA, covering rubric topics
  [ ] Issues created from feedback (peer team, TA, supervisor, instructor,
      self) on Problem Statement, PoC plan, Dev Plan, Team Charter - each
      unambiguous, detailed, actionable, clear close condition, one-person
      sized
  [ ] Full team attends, engages, brings questions, and accepts feedback in
      the informal TA presentations (SRS and HA)
