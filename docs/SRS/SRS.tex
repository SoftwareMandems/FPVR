\booksection{G}{Goals}

\subsection{Context and Overall Objectives}\label{g1}

Learning to operate a first-person-view (FPV) drone presents challenges for beginners, particularly when practicing with physical equipment. New pilots must develop coordinated flight-control skills while managing the possibility of crashes, equipment damage, and unsafe flight conditions. These challenges can make initial training costly and discourage inexperienced users from gaining practical experience.

FPVR is a virtual reality (VR)-based FPV drone training simulator designed to provide a safe, accessible, and structured environment for developing foundational drone piloting skills. The system will allow users to control a simulated drone, practice flight manoeuvres, and complete progressively challenging courses without requiring a physical drone.

The training environment will include free-flight practice, multiple obstacle-course levels, distinct virtual environments, checkpoints, and performance evaluation. Users will receive 
feedback on their flight performance through measurable indicators such as completion time, checkpoint progress, and performance ratings. 
An intelligent feedback component may provide further analysis of user performance.

FPVR will also support registered player accounts,
persistent performance records, and local and global
leaderboards. Users will be able to practise without
signing in, while registered players can save their
completed results to an online service, review
previous attempts, and compare their performance
with other players. These features extend the
training experience beyond individual sessions
by providing measurable records of progress.



\subsection{Current situation}\label{g2}

Learning to operate a first-person-view (FPV) drone requires beginners to develop coordinated control skills while interpreting visual feedback from the drone's perspective. Practicing with physical drones introduces risks of crashes, equipment damage, and additional costs, making simulation a valuable alternative for introductory training.

Several established FPV drone simulators, including Liftoff: FPV Drone Racing~\cite{liftoffFPV}  and VelociDrone\cite{velocidrone}, allow users to practice flight manoeuvres, complete racing courses, and explore virtual environments through conventional PC-based interfaces. These platforms provide extensive training and recreational capabilities, with a strong emphasis on realistic flight behaviour, racing, and freestyle flying. However, their traditional desktop experiences do not provide the same interaction environment as a dedicated virtual reality (VR) headset.

VR-based alternatives also exist. Liftoff: XR Drone~\cite{liftoffXR} introduces accessible drone flight using Meta Quest controllers, while DVR Simulator~\cite{dvrSimulator} and Drone Blitz~\cite{droneBlitz} offer VR flight challenges and racing or performance-tracking features. These applications demonstrate that immersive drone simulation is already feasible, but they differ in their emphasis on casual exploration, racing, and structured practice.

FPVR aims to build on these existing approaches by focusing specifically on a progressive, beginner-oriented VR training experience. Rather than treating flight practice and course completion as isolated activities, FPVR intends to connect free-flight familiarization, increasingly challenging obstacle courses, checkpoint-based objectives, and post-flight performance reviews into a continuous learning process. By allowing users to evaluate their results and repeat challenges, the system aims to help beginners recognize their mistakes and track improvements over successive attempts.

The intended contribution of FPVR is therefore not the introduction of drone simulation or VR flight itself, but the integration of immersive flight practice with structured progression and measurable feedback. The effectiveness of this approach will require evaluation with potential users.

\subsection{Expected Benefits}\label{g3}

\textbf{Safe and Repeatable Practice (G1):}
FPVR will allow users to practice drone operation without exposing
a real drone or surrounding property to collision-related damage.
Users can recover from simulated crashes and repeat exercises
without the costs associated with damaging physical equipment.

\medskip

\textbf{Progressive Skill Development (G2):}
Rather than relying exclusively on unrestricted flight, FPVR
will offer structured challenges of varying difficulty.
Users can freely select levels based on their experience and
practice increasingly demanding manoeuvres at their own pace.

\medskip

\textbf{Performance Awareness (G3):}
After completing a training level, users will be able to
review their performance through recorded measures such as
completion time, checkpoint progress, and collisions.
This feedback is intended to help users identify areas for
improvement and encourage deliberate practice.

\medskip

\textbf{Accessible Introductory Training (G4):}
FPVR will accommodate users with limited or no prior FPV
flying experience by providing a free-flight environment
where they can become familiar with flight controls before
attempting structured challenges.

\medskip

\hypertarget{goal:G5}{}\textbf{Persistent Progress and
Performance Comparison (G5):}
FPVR will allow registered users to retain
their completed flight results across sessions
and access them from supported installations.
Local and global leaderboards will provide
opportunities to compare performance on
individual obstacle-course levels, encouraging
users to revisit challenges and track their
improvement over time.

\medskip

These benefits concern the intended training experience.
Any claim that FPVR improves actual physical-drone piloting
ability will require separate validation and is not assumed
by this specification.

\subsection{High-level usage scenarios}\label{g5}

This section describes the primary interactions between
users and FPVR during flight practice, obstacle-course
completion, crash recovery, and performance evaluation.
These scenarios illustrate the intended system behaviour
and support the goals defined in Section G.1.

\subsubsection{Scenario 1: Initial Free-Flight Practice}

A first-time user launches FPVR and selects the free-flight
mode using either Meta Quest controllers or a compatible
Xbox controller. The user enters a virtual environment
where they can practice basic flight manoeuvres, such as
ascending, descending, turning, and navigating through
the environment.

Unlike structured obstacle-course levels, free-flight
mode has no checkpoints, timers, or scoring. Users can
explore and practice at their own pace without specific
completion objectives.

If the drone experiences a simulated crash, the user can
reset its position and continue practicing. The user may
leave free-flight mode and return to the level-selection
interface when desired.

This scenario supports G1 and G4 by providing a safe,
repeatable environment for becoming familiar with FPV
drone controls.


\subsubsection{Scenario 2: Completing an Obstacle-Course Level}

A user selects an obstacle-course level from the available
challenges. All levels are accessible regardless of previous
completion, allowing users to choose courses appropriate
to their experience and preferences.

Each level contains obstacles and a sequence of checkpoints
that must be completed in the designated order. The system
tracks the user's progression, total elapsed time, and time
spent navigating between checkpoints. Upon reaching a
checkpoint, the current section timer resets while the
total elapsed time continues accumulating.

The user navigates through the checkpoints in sequence
until reaching the finish. After successful completion,
the system calculates a numerical score and a separate
performance rating based on the recorded flight results.

The user can then review their performance, retry the
level, or select another challenge.

This scenario supports G2 by providing structured flight
challenges of varying difficulty with measurable outcomes.


\subsubsection{Scenario 3: Recovering from a Simulated Crash}

While attempting an obstacle-course level, the user loses
control of the drone and experiences a simulated crash.
The system records the collision and provides two recovery
options: restart the entire level or respawn at the most
recently reached checkpoint.

If the user selects respawn, the drone returns to the last
successfully reached checkpoint. If no checkpoints have
been reached, the drone returns to the level's starting
position.

The current section timer resets upon respawning, allowing
the user to attempt the section again. However, the total
elapsed time continues accumulating, including the time
spent on failed attempts. The recorded collision also
negatively affects the final score.

The user can continue attempting the course without
restarting the FPVR application.

This scenario supports G1 and G2 by allowing users to
recover from mistakes while maintaining meaningful
performance consequences.

\subsubsection{Scenario 4: Reviewing Flight Performance}

After successfully completing an obstacle-course level,
the user receives a flight review summarizing their
performance.

The review includes the total completion time, checkpoint
results, collision count, and numerical score. The total
completion time includes time spent on failed sections
and subsequent attempts following checkpoint respawns.

The system also presents a separate overall performance
rating based on defined evaluation criteria. The user can
examine these results to identify areas for improvement
before attempting another flight challenge.

This scenario supports G3 by providing measurable feedback
intended to encourage deliberate practice and improvement.

The exact scoring formula, rating categories, and penalties
associated with collisions must be established in \hyperref[s2]{S.2}.


\subsubsection{Scenario 5: AI-Assisted Flight Feedback
(Planned: Subject to Feasibility)}

If AI-assisted feedback is included in the final
implementation, the system will automatically provide
additional performance analysis as part of the post-level
flight review.

Using available flight-performance data, the system would
identify areas of difficulty and provide understandable
observations or recommendations. For example, the
feedback might highlight repeated collisions or
difficulties navigating particular obstacle sections.

The intelligent feedback component is intended to
complement the standard numerical score and performance
rating rather than replace them.

The feasibility, data requirements, analysis approach,
and evaluation criteria for AI-assisted feedback remain
to be determined. The standard flight review will remain
available independently of this optional capability.

\subsubsection{Scenario 6: Account Registration
and Performance Tracking}

A user launches FPVR and chooses to register
a player account using a username and password.
After successfully registering or signing in,
the user selects an obstacle-course level and
completes a flight challenge.

Upon completion, FPVR presents the standard
post-flight review, including the completion
time, collision count, numerical score, and
performance rating. The result is saved locally
and, when the online service is available,
synchronized with the user's registered account.

The user may later sign in on another supported
installation to retrieve previously synchronized
results and review their performance over
multiple attempts.

This scenario supports G3 and G5 by enabling
persistent performance records and allowing
users to monitor their improvement over time.


\subsubsection{Scenario 7: Viewing and Comparing
Leaderboard Results}

After completing an obstacle-course level,
a user opens the leaderboard for that level.

FPVR displays a local leaderboard containing
results saved on the current installation.
When the online service is available, the
user can also view a global leaderboard
containing results submitted by registered
players.

The leaderboards rank results according to
numerical score, with completion time and
completion timestamp used to resolve ties.
Users can compare their performance with
other recorded attempts and repeat the level
to improve their ranking.

If the online service is unavailable, the
local leaderboard remains accessible, and
the user can continue training normally.

This scenario supports G3 and G5 by providing
measurable performance comparisons and
encouraging repeated practice.


\subsection{Limitations and Exclusions}\label{g6}

FPVR is intended to provide an introductory virtual reality
(VR) training experience for first-person-view (FPV) drone
operation. The following limitations and exclusions define
the boundaries of the system and clarify functionality
outside the project's current scope.

\newpage
\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{FPVR Limitations and Exclusions}
\label{tab:limitations-exclusions}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{
    @{}p{4cm} X@{}
}
\toprule
\textbf{Area} &
\textbf{Limitation or Exclusion} \\
\midrule

Physical Drone Operation &
FPVR is limited to simulated drone operation and will not
communicate with or control physical drones. \\

\addlinespace

VR Hardware Requirement &
FPVR requires a compatible VR headset for operation.
Both Meta Quest controllers and compatible Xbox controllers
will be supported, but conventional PC gameplay without
a VR headset is excluded. \\

\addlinespace

Pilot Certification &
FPVR will not provide official drone pilot certification
or replace applicable regulatory examinations, assessments,
or licensing requirements. \\

\addlinespace

Real-World Flight Proficiency &
Performance within FPVR will not be considered proof
of a user's ability to safely operate a physical drone.
The transfer of skills from simulation to real-world
flight is not guaranteed. \\

\addlinespace

Flight Simulation Realism &
FPVR aims to provide realistic flight behaviour for
training purposes. However, the underlying flight model,
its implementation, and the required level of accuracy
remain under review. The system is not intended to
reproduce every physical and environmental characteristic
of real-world drone operation. \\

\addlinespace

Multiplayer Functionality &
The core FPVR system is limited to single-player training.
Multiplayer flight, in which multiple users operate drones
in a shared virtual environment, is a stretch goal and
will be implemented only if development time permits. \\

\addlinespace

Dedicated FPV Transmitters &
Support for dedicated FPV radio transmitters is under
consideration but is not part of the confirmed core
controller requirements. Compatibility and feasibility
will be evaluated during development. \\

\addlinespace


Online Service Dependency &
Account registration, global leaderboards, and
cloud synchronization require network connectivity
and an available online service. Core training
activities, local result storage, and standard
post-flight reviews remain available without
these online services. \\


\addlinespace

AI-Assisted Feedback &
AI-generated flight feedback is planned subject to
feasibility evaluation. Core flight reviews, numerical
scores, and performance ratings will remain available
independently of this optional capability. \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

structured obstacle-course challenges, and performance
evaluation within a virtual environment. The system
is not intended to replace real-world flight training
or serve as a comprehensive engineering-grade flight
simulator. The risks associated with these limitations and their corresponding
mitigation strategies are discussed in
\hyperref[p6]{P.6}.

\subsection{User Profiles}\label{g8}

FPVR is intended for users with varying levels of
experience in drone operation, virtual reality (VR),
and video games. The system primarily targets
beginners while also accommodating recreational
users and experienced FPV pilots. Users must comply
with the age and safety restrictions of the
supported VR hardware.

The following profiles describe the anticipated
experience, needs, and usage patterns of the
system's main user groups.


\subsubsection{Novice FPV Pilot}
\label{sec:novice-user}

The primary user is an individual with little or
no experience operating first-person-view (FPV)
drones. This user may also be unfamiliar with
VR environments and their associated controllers.

\noindent\textbf{Experience:}
May have general familiarity with computers or
video games but limited knowledge of drone flight
controls, navigation, and flight behaviour.

\noindent\textbf{Needs:}
Clear control instructions, understandable
interactions, opportunities to practise without
risking damage to physical equipment, and
feedback on flight performance.

\noindent\textbf{Expected Usage:}
The user reviews the basic control instructions
and begins practising in free-flight mode.
As confidence improves, the user may select
obstacle-course levels of increasing difficulty.
Progression is voluntary, as all levels are
available without prerequisite completion.
The user may initially practise without creating
an account. If they choose to register, they
can preserve their completed results and review
previous attempts to evaluate their progress
over time.


\subsubsection{Casual Gamer or Technology Enthusiast}
\label{sec:casual-user}

This user is interested in experiencing drone
flight through VR without necessarily intending
to purchase or operate a physical drone.

\noindent\textbf{Experience:}
May be familiar with video-game controllers and
immersive experiences but have limited knowledge
of actual FPV drone operation.

\noindent\textbf{Needs:}
Engaging virtual environments, accessible
controls, understandable objectives, and
opportunities to measure improvement through
repeated challenges.

\noindent\textbf{Expected Usage:}
The user explores free-flight environments
and attempts obstacle-course challenges for
recreation and exploration. The user may
repeat levels to improve completion times,
scores, and performance ratings. 
The user may also create an account to
participate in global leaderboards and compare
their results with other players. Local
leaderboards provide an additional way to
compare attempts recorded on the same
installation.



\subsubsection{Experienced FPV Pilot or Technical Evaluator}
\label{sec:experienced-user}

This user has prior experience operating physical
FPV drones or working with related technical
systems. Experienced users may participate in
regular training activities or provide feedback
during system evaluation.

\noindent\textbf{Experience:}
Familiar with drone controls, flight manoeuvres,
and the expected behaviour of physical drones.

\noindent\textbf{Needs:}
Consistent control responses, meaningful
obstacle-course challenges, and sufficient
performance information to evaluate the
simulator's behaviour.

\noindent\textbf{Expected Usage:}
The user practises in free-flight mode,
completes obstacle-course challenges, and
reviews recorded performance results.
The user may also compare simulated flight
behaviour with prior experience and identify
areas for improvement. The required accuracy
of the underlying flight model remains
under review.
Registered experienced users may also review
historical results and compare their performance
across repeated attempts. These comparisons
may support evaluation of the simulator's
consistency, but do not establish real-world
flight proficiency.



\subsubsection{General User Considerations}
\label{sec:general-user-considerations}

FPVR is intended to accommodate both seated and
standing operation using a compatible VR headset.
Users may interact with the simulator through
Meta Quest controllers or compatible Xbox
controllers.

Users may differ in physical capabilities,
experience with immersive environments, and
susceptibility to VR-related discomfort.
The system's design will consider applicable
headset safety guidance and the needs of
different user groups.

The profiles presented in this section are
based on the intended target audience rather
than completed user interviews. As discussed
in Section~\ref{g7},
the project team plans to consult potential
users to validate their needs, expectations,
and usage assumptions.

\subsection{Invariants}\label{e5}

An invariant is a rule that must stay true while FPVR operates. Values such as
elapsed time and checkpoint count can change, but they must change according
to these rules. The rules below explain the required behaviour and link to the
requirements that define it.

\begin{description}
\item[{\hyperref[et-scope]{E-I-01}:}]\phantomsection\label{ei-boundary}
\textbf{Simulated flight only.}
FPVR controls a simulated drone. No action, including moving the throttle or
recovering from a crash, may send a command to a physical aircraft. A score or
rating also does not certify real-world piloting ability (\hyperref[g6]{G.6}).
\textit{Reason:} users need to understand the limits of simulator practice.

\item[{\hyperref[et-course]{E-I-02}:}]
\phantomsection\label{ei-course}
\textbf{Checkpoint order and recovery.}
Completed checkpoints must always form the beginning of the course's required
sequence, with none skipped. Respawn keeps progress in the same attempt;
restart begins a new attempt.

\textbf{Checkpoint order.} Let $n$ be the number of required checkpoints (a positive integer),
listed in order as $\langle C_1,\ldots,C_n\rangle$. Let $k$ count the completed
checkpoints and $P$ list them. Then
\[
  k\in\{0,\ldots,n\},\qquad P=\langle C_1,\ldots,C_k\rangle.
\]
This says that $k$ cannot be below zero or above $n$, and $P$ contains exactly
the first $k$ checkpoints. Before the first checkpoint, $k=0$ and $P$ is empty.

During active flight, when no crash awaits recovery, let $e$ be a checkpoint
passage. A prime ($'$) means the value just after that event:
\[
 k'=\begin{cases}
 k+1, & k<n\ \text{and }e\text{ is passage through }C_{k+1},\\
 k, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Only the next required checkpoint increases progress. Repeated or out-of-order
checkpoints do not count, and progress cannot advance while a crash awaits
recovery (\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-06},
\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-01--03}). Reaching the finish counts as
completion only after all $n$ checkpoints are complete
(\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-08}).

\textit{Example, using three checkpoints:} after $C_1$, $k=1$ and
$P=\langle C_1\rangle$. Passing $C_3$ next changes neither value. Passing $C_2$
then gives $k=2$ and $P=\langle C_1,C_2\rangle$. The user must still pass $C_3$
and reach the finish. These numbers illustrate the rule; they do not set the
number of checkpoints in every course.

\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\textbf{Crash recovery.} Respawn continues the current attempt; restart starts
again from the beginning. Table~\ref{tab:e-recovery} shows exactly what is kept
or reset (\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-05--07}).

\small\captionsetup{hypcap=false}
\captionof{table}{Respawn and restart}\label{tab:e-recovery}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.25\linewidth}YY@{}}\toprule
\textbf{Item} & \textbf{Respawn} & \textbf{Restart}\\\midrule

\textit{Example:} checkpoints $C_1$ and $C_2$ are complete, total flight time is
42 seconds, and one crash has been counted. Respawn returns the drone to $C_2$
and keeps both checkpoints, the 42 seconds, the crash count and its penalty.
The current section timer resets to zero. These values illustrate recovery;
they are not performance targets.

\textbf{Timing.} Failed flight still counts toward total time. Time spent in
the crash-recovery menu does not. Let $T$ be total flight time and $\Delta t$
be a non-negative elapsed interval, both measured in seconds. The total after
that interval is $T'$:
\[
 T'=\begin{cases}
 T+\Delta t, & \text{during active flight},\\
 T, & \text{while the crash-recovery menu is displayed}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For example, spending ten seconds in that menu does not add ten seconds to
$T$. Respawn keeps $T$; restart resets it. The section timer resets when the
next checkpoint is completed or the drone respawns. Each new crash adds one to
the crash count (\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-01--07}). Total time
stops at successful completion (\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-10}).
\textit{Reason:} respawn must not erase failed-flight time or crash penalties;
restart begins a new attempt.
These course rules do not apply to free flight, which has no checkpoints,
timers or score.

\item[{\hyperref[et-safety]{E-I-03}:}]\phantomsection\label{ei-stationary}
\textbf{Stationary interaction.}
Users must be able to control flight, use menus, recover and exit while seated
or standing. Flying through a virtual course must not require walking through
the physical room. Exit must be accessible without removing the headset
(\hyperref[sec:s2-interface]{FR-UI-06},
\hyperref[sec:s2-nfr]{NFR-SAFE-01}).
\textit{Reason:} the controls must suit the prepared stationary space, including
after a simulated crash.

\item[{\hyperref[et-review]{E-I-04}:}]\phantomsection\label{ei-review}
\textbf{Standard review without optional analysis.}
After successful completion, the user must receive the current attempt's time,
checkpoint results, crash count, score and rating. This review remains available
whether intelligent feedback is not included, succeeds, is unavailable or fails.
For example, an analysis error must still allow the user to review the results
and retry the course (\hyperref[sec:s2-review]{FR-REV-01--04},
\hyperref[sec:s2-ai]{FR-AI-03}).
\textit{Reason:} an optional feature must not block the core training experience.
\end{description}

The FPVR system is a VR-based first-person-view (FPV) drone simulation game.

\subsection{Functionality}\label{s2}

This section defines the externally observable functional and non-functional requirements of FPVR. Each requirement has a unique identifier and an individual rationale. Requirements apply to the initial release unless explicitly designated as an optional enhancement. The target headset is Meta Quest 3S. Numeric thresholds and scoring parameters in this section constitute a proposed, testable baseline to be approved by the project team and verified on target hardware.

\subsubsection{Drone Flight Simulation}
\label{sec:s2-flight}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-01:} FPVR shall display a first-person view whose position and orientation follow the simulated drone during flight.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The view must correspond to the vehicle being controlled to provide an FPV training experience.
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-02:} FPVR shall accept independently variable throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw inputs and produce a corresponding change in simulated drone behaviour for each input.\\
\textit{Rationale:} These are the four primary control axes expected in manual FPV flight.
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-03:} When the drone is airborne and no upward thrust is applied, its vertical velocity shall decrease under simulated gravity.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Gravity is necessary to distinguish the flight model from unrestricted game-style movement and can be checked without prescribing a physics engine.
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-04:} FPVR shall detect drone contact with course obstacles and ground surfaces during flight.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Contact detection is necessary to enforce obstacle challenges and initiate recovery.
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-05:} Initial ground contact before the first takeoff of an attempt shall not count as a crash; contact with the ground after the drone has become airborne, or contact with a designated obstacle at any time, shall trigger a simulated crash.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This permits conventional takeoff while making the collision rule unambiguous. For acceptance testing, the airborne state begins when the drone is no longer in contact with its starting ground surface.
\item \textbf{FR-FLT-06:} Each crash shall produce exactly one crash event, suspend normal flight input, and present the recovery behaviour applicable to the current mode.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A single event prevents a prolonged collision from generating duplicate penalties.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Training Modes and Level Management}
\label{sec:s2-levels}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-01:} FPVR shall provide free-flight practice without checkpoints, a completion timer, numerical scoring, or performance ratings.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Beginners need an unrestricted setting to learn the controls before attempting assessed challenges.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-02:} Following a crash in free-flight mode, the user shall be able to restore the drone to its free-flight starting position without restarting FPVR.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Quick recovery enables repeated practice without interruption.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-03:} FPVR shall provide at least three selectable obstacle-course levels, each with a distinct obstacle or checkpoint arrangement; the levels shall be identified as introductory, intermediate, and advanced.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Three differentiated configurations establish a verifiable minimum for varied practice without relying on subjective difficulty claims.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-04:} FPVR shall provide at least two visually distinct virtual environments, with at least one playable obstacle-course level in each.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Distinct environments offer variation while permitting reuse of an environment by multiple levels.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-05:} Every obstacle-course level shall be selectable immediately without prerequisites or unlock conditions.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users with different skill levels should choose their own training difficulty.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-06:} The user shall be able to leave either training mode and return to the main menu without restarting the application.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users must be able to stop a VR activity without terminating the software.
\item \textbf{FR-LVL-07:} Starting an obstacle-course attempt shall initialize checkpoint progress to zero, both timers to zero, crash count to zero, and the attempt's score state to its initial value.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A fresh attempt must be independent of the previous attempt's performance.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Checkpoints and Crash Recovery}
\label{sec:s2-checkpoints}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-01:} Each obstacle-course level shall have an ordered list of at least two checkpoints followed by a finish location.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Ordered segments are needed for measurable progression and checkpoint-based practice.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-02:} FPVR shall record a checkpoint as completed only when the drone passes through the next checkpoint in the prescribed order.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This makes completion dependent on following the intended course.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-03:} Passing through a later checkpoint before completing the next required checkpoint shall not advance progress.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This prevents bypassing the challenge sequence.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-04:} After a crash in an obstacle course, FPVR shall display the choices \emph{Respawn} and \emph{Restart Level}.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users need both targeted practice and the option to begin a fresh scored attempt.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-05:} Respawn shall return the drone to the most recently completed checkpoint, or to the level start if none has been completed.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The player can retry the failed segment without losing earlier valid progress.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-06:} Respawn shall preserve completed checkpoints, accumulated total flight time, and the recorded crash count and score penalties.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Repeated failed attempts must continue to affect the overall performance result.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-07:} Restart Level shall return the drone to the level start and reset checkpoints, both timers, crash count, and score state.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Restarting is defined as a new attempt rather than a shortcut that retains previous penalties.
\item \textbf{FR-CHK-08:} FPVR shall declare a level complete only after every checkpoint has been completed in order and the drone reaches the finish.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This gives a deterministic and testable definition of successful completion.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Timing and Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:s2-performance}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-PER-01:} Each obstacle-course attempt shall have a total timer that measures accumulated active flight time from the start of the attempt to successful completion.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A single total time provides a comparable performance measure for a completed attempt.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-02:} The total timer shall retain time spent in flight segments that end in crashes.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Failed attempts must not disappear from the overall completion-time result.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-03:} Each obstacle-course attempt shall have a separate section timer measuring active flight time since the start of the current checkpoint segment.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Segment timing provides immediate feedback during repeated practice.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-04:} The section timer shall reset to zero when the next required checkpoint is completed and when a checkpoint respawn occurs.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Each segment attempt should begin with a fresh local timer.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-05:} Checkpoint respawn shall not reset the total timer; Restart Level shall reset both timers.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The two recovery choices intentionally have different effects on attempt history.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-06:} Both timers shall pause while the crash-recovery menu is displayed and resume when active flight resumes; no menu time shall be added to either timer.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Decision time in a menu should not affect flight performance.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-07:} FPVR shall increment the crash count once for each registered crash event in an obstacle-course attempt.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The count is required for meaningful penalties and a transparent review.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-08:} At level completion, FPVR shall calculate a whole-number score as $\max(0,1000-\lfloor T\rfloor-100C)$, where $T$ is total active flight time in seconds and $C$ is the number of registered crashes.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This proposed baseline is deterministic, easy to reproduce in tests, and penalizes both time and crashes. The constants are initial game-balance choices, not experimentally validated measures of pilot skill.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-09:} FPVR shall assign the rating Gold for scores of at least 800, Silver for 600--799, Bronze for 400--599, and Needs Improvement for 0--399.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Fixed non-overlapping score bands make ratings unambiguous and verifiable; the bands are initial design choices rather than validated proficiency levels.
\item \textbf{FR-PER-10:} The total timer shall stop when the level is successfully completed, and its final value shall be used in the score and post-flight review.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A fixed completion event ensures consistent reported results.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{VR Interface and Controller Input}
\label{sec:s2-interface}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-UI-01:} FPVR shall present the flight view and training menus in a Meta Quest 3S headset.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The Quest 3S is the agreed primary evaluation device.
\item \textbf{FR-UI-02:} FPVR shall support both standalone execution on Quest 3S and PCVR execution with Quest 3S connected to a compatible Windows 11 computer.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Both deployment paths are in the project scope; Windows 11 is the proposed reference PCVR operating system for repeatable testing.
\item \textbf{FR-UI-03:} FPVR shall provide a flight-control configuration for Quest 3S motion controllers in standalone mode and an Xbox-compatible gamepad in PCVR mode, each providing throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This defines a minimum verifiable controller-to-execution-mode matrix without promising every controller in every mode.
\item \textbf{FR-UI-04:} For each supported controller configuration, FPVR shall display a control guide identifying the assigned throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw inputs before the user begins flight.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Explicit instructions allow users to operate the simulator without assuming previous FPV experience.
\item \textbf{FR-UI-05:} FPVR shall provide menu interactions for choosing training mode, selecting a level, starting flight, recovering from crashes, and returning to the main menu.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Every required gameplay transition must be accessible to the user.
\item \textbf{FR-UI-06:} FPVR shall permit seated and standing operation without requiring the user to physically walk to control drone movement.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users should be able to operate the simulator within a stationary VR play area.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Player Accounts and Authentication}
\label{sec:s2-accounts}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-ACC-01:} FPVR shall allow a user with network connectivity to register an account using a unique username and password.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Persistent identity is needed to associate results with a player and support global ranking.
\item \textbf{FR-ACC-02:} FPVR shall reject registration when the username is already registered or the password contains fewer than 12 characters, and shall display an explanatory message.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Duplicate identity must be prevented, and a clear minimum password rule is easier to verify than an undefined strong-password requirement.
\item \textbf{FR-ACC-03:} FPVR shall allow a registered user to sign in with valid credentials and shall reject invalid credentials without disclosing whether the username or password was incorrect.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Authentication protects ownership of stored results while avoiding unnecessary account enumeration.
\item \textbf{FR-ACC-04:} FPVR shall provide a sign-out action that ends the current authenticated session on that installation.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Shared headsets or computers require a way to stop acting under another player's identity.
\item \textbf{FR-ACC-05:} FPVR shall allow free-flight and obstacle-course practice without an authenticated account, but shall require authentication before submitting a result to the global leaderboard.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Account or network failure should not block the core training experience, while public rankings need attributable submissions.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Result Storage and Leaderboards}
\label{sec:s2-leaderboard}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-01:} After a completed obstacle-course attempt, FPVR shall save a local result record containing the level identifier, completion timestamp, total time, crash count, score, rating, and the active local player identifier.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A local record supports a device leaderboard and retains results when a network connection is unavailable.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-02:} For an authenticated player with network connectivity, FPVR shall submit the completed result to the online result service and associate it with that player's account.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Cloud association allows results to be retrieved across installations and included in global rankings.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-03:} FPVR shall retain a locally saved result when an online submission fails and shall allow the player to retry synchronization after connectivity is restored.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Temporary network failure should not erase a completed attempt.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-04:} FPVR shall display a local leaderboard for each obstacle-course level using results saved on the current installation.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users can compare results without relying on an external service.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-05:} FPVR shall display a global leaderboard for each obstacle-course level using results successfully accepted by the online service.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Online rankings enable comparison between registered players across installations.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-06:} Both leaderboards shall rank results by descending numerical score, using shorter total completion time as the first tiebreaker and earlier completion timestamp as the second tiebreaker.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A complete ordering rule prevents ambiguous rankings for equal scores.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-07:} Each leaderboard shall show at least the top ten available results for the selected level, including rank, player display name, score, completion time, and crash count.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A fixed minimum display capacity and field list make the leaderboard interface testable.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-08:} When the online leaderboard is unavailable, FPVR shall display an availability message and continue to provide the local leaderboard and training modes.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Online-service failure must not disable core gameplay.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-09:} A registered player shall be able to retrieve their previously synchronized completed results after signing in on another supported installation.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Cross-device retrieval is the user-visible benefit of cloud-backed result persistence.
\item \textbf{FR-DAT-10:} FPVR shall distinguish results saved only locally from results successfully synchronized to the online service.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users must not be misled into believing an offline result is already included in the global ranking.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Post-Flight Review}
\label{sec:s2-review}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-REV-01:} FPVR shall display a post-flight review immediately after successful completion of an obstacle-course level.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Immediate feedback connects performance outcomes to the just-completed attempt.
\item \textbf{FR-REV-02:} The review shall display the completed level, total completion time, completed checkpoints, crash count, numerical score, and rating.\\
\textit{Rationale:} These measures explain the result and support deliberate practice.
\item \textbf{FR-REV-03:} From the review, the user shall be able to retry the same level, choose another level, or return to the main menu.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users should be able to continue training without restarting the application.
\item \textbf{FR-REV-04:} The standard review shall be displayed even when the network or intelligent feedback service is unavailable.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Core performance feedback must not depend on optional external services.
\item \textbf{FR-REV-05:} FPVR shall permit registered players to view their stored completed results from prior sessions, showing at least the fields specified in FR-DAT-01.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Account-linked result persistence is useful only if users can retrieve and inspect their earlier performance; this supersedes the earlier exclusion of historical results.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Intelligent Feedback} 
\label{sec:s2-ai}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-AI-01:} FPVR shall generate a natural-language observation based on the completed attempt's available time, checkpoint, and crash data.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Feedback must be grounded in measurable attempt data rather than invented flight events.
\item \textbf{FR-AI-02:} FPVR shall display it automatically in the post-flight review without requiring an additional user request.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Automatic presentation makes the enhancement accessible during the normal review workflow.
\item \textbf{FR-AI-03:} Failure or unavailability of intelligent feedback shall not prevent display of the standard post-flight review.\\
\textit{Rationale:} An add-on feature must not become a single point of failure for required feedback.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Non-Functional Requirements}
\label{sec:s2-nfr}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NFR-INST-01 (Installability):} FPVR shall install and launch as a standalone application on Meta Quest 3S without a connected PC.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Standalone operation is a committed delivery mode, not merely an editor demonstration.
\item \textbf{NFR-INST-02 (Installability):} FPVR shall launch in PCVR mode on a Windows 11 computer connected to a Quest 3S using a supported PCVR connection method.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A specified reference operating system makes the second deployment mode independently testable.
\item \textbf{NFR-COMP-01 (Compatibility):} Quest 3S motion controllers shall support the four flight axes in standalone mode, and an Xbox-compatible gamepad shall support the four flight axes in PCVR mode.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This minimum matrix is testable without promising unsupported controller combinations.
\item \textbf{NFR-PERF-01 (Rendering):} On Quest 3S in standalone mode, FPVR shall maintain an average of at least 72 rendered frames per second over a continuous 120-second normal-flight test in each of the two required environments.\\
\textit{Rationale:} The 72-FPS threshold is an initial headset-oriented performance target; averaging over a fixed interval makes the result reproducible, although frame-time spikes should also be inspected during V\&V.
\item \textbf{NFR-PERF-02 (Input Response):} In the reference test environment, the elapsed time from a received flight-control input event to the first corresponding simulated state update shall not exceed 100 ms in at least 95\% of 100 measured input events.\\
\textit{Rationale:} This proposed software-response threshold makes responsiveness measurable without conflating it with headset display latency.
\item \textbf{NFR-REL-01 (Reliability):} FPVR shall complete 20 consecutive crash-and-respawn cycles in each required obstacle-course environment without application termination or loss of checkpoint consistency.\\
\textit{Rationale:} A repeatable stress test checks the recovery workflow that users will exercise frequently.
\item \textbf{NFR-USAB-01 (Usability):} The control guide shall be accessible from the main menu and before the first flight of a newly launched session.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Beginners need discoverable instructions without requiring prior experience.
\item \textbf{NFR-SAFE-01 (VR Safety):} FPVR shall allow the user to pause or leave an active flight activity using an in-application menu without removing the headset.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Users must be able to stop an uncomfortable experience promptly.
\item \textbf{NFR-SAFE-02 (Stationary Use):} All core flight, menu, and recovery actions shall be operable while seated or standing in one physical location.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Stationary operation reduces the need for movement within the user's physical surroundings.
\item \textbf{NFR-SEC-01 (Credential Protection):} FPVR shall transmit account credentials only over encrypted connections and shall not store account passwords in plaintext on the device or online service.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Online account functionality introduces credential exposure risks absent from the original offline-only simulator.
\item \textbf{NFR-SEC-02 (Access Control):} An unauthenticated user shall not be able to retrieve another player's non-public account data or submit a leaderboard result under another player's account identifier.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Public rankings must not compromise account privacy or permit identity spoofing.
\item \textbf{NFR-DAT-01 (Persistence):} A result confirmed as synchronized shall remain retrievable after the player signs out, restarts FPVR, and signs in again.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Persistence across sessions is the defining acceptance condition for cloud-backed storage.
\item \textbf{NFR-OFF-01 (Offline Availability):} Free-flight, obstacle-course play, crash recovery, local result saving, and standard post-flight review shall remain usable when the online service is unavailable.\\
\textit{Rationale:} Core training should remain available despite network failures; account registration, global ranking, and synchronization may require connectivity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interfaces}\label{s3}

% \guidance{How the system makes the functionality of \hyperref[s2]{S.2} available to the rest of the world, particularly user interfaces and program interfaces (APIs). It specifies how that functionality will be made available to the rest of the world, including people (users) and other systems. These are interfaces provided by the system to the outside; the other way around, interfaces from other systems, which the system may use, are specified in \hyperref[e2]{E.2}. \cite{BM22}}

\begin{description}
\item[IF-01: Entry and navigation:] The VR and nunchuck controllers provide access to a main menu where
users can select modes for free flight, available course choices, and return/exit actions. (\req{FR-UI-04}/\req{FR-UI-05},
\req{FR-LVL-05}/\req{FR-LVL-06}).
\item[IF-02: Flight:] Continuous throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw actions affect
the drone's first-person view based on inputs from a controller (\req{FR-FLT-02}). The application will display progress during
a course (\req{FR-REV-02}), where as free flight has no course timer, checkpoints, or score
(\req{FR-FLT-01}/\req{FR-FLT-02}, \req{FR-LVL-01}).
\item[IF-03: Retry:] Upon failure to complete a course due to a crash or timeout, users can press a button on the controller to reset the drone to a prior checkpoint.
Selection is an explicit menu action that can be interfaced with a controller.
(\req{FR-LVL-02}, \req{FR-CHK-04}--\req{FR-CHK-07};).
\item[IF-04: Review:] On course completion show the selected course, final total
active time labelled in seconds, checkpoint results in course order, integer
crash count, numerical score in points, and separate rating. Retry and activity
selection remain available; prior-session navigation is absent. Unapproved
scoring parameters must be identified as provisional in any prototype rather
than presented as validated training results (\req{FR-REV-01}--\req{FR-REV-05}).
\item[IF-05: Platform Interaction:] Consumes pose/input availability and focus;
produces frames and lifecycle responses through the supported runtime. The
proposed unavailable-input presentation is descriptive and recoverable where
the runtime permits (\req{SR-02}). There is no promise of public
network APIs, desktop-only controls, or physical-drone interfaces.
\item[IF-06: AI Feedback:] The feedback provided by the AI system receives only separately approved
current-attempt data and advises on possible places for improvement. The
standard review is immediate and independent; an analysis may join it
automatically if the optional capability is approved
(\req{FR-AI-01}--\req{FR-AI-03}, \req{SR-08}). Output schemas,
provider, timeout, and data permission are \textbf{TO BE REVIEWED} before external
AI enters scope.
\end{description}
Interfaces describe visible information and action meanings. Exact widget
layout, engine classes, storage structures, and input packages remain design
choices. Proposed safety/error interactions are subject to their SR status.

\subsection{Detailed usage scenarios}\label{s4}

% \guidance{Examples of interaction between the environment (or human users) and the system, expressed as user stories. Such scenarios are not by themselves a substitute for precise descriptions of functionality (\hyperref[s3]{S.3}), but provide an important complement by specifying cases that these behavior descriptions must support; they also serve as a basis for developing test cases. The scenarios most relevant for stakeholders are given in chapter \hyperref[g5]{G.5} in the Goals book, at a general level, as use cases; in contrast, \hyperref[s4]{S.4} can refer to system components and functionality (from other chapters of the System book) as well as special and erroneous cases, and introduce more specific scenarios. \cite{BM22}}

\begin{description}
\item[U-01: Start a Free-Flight Session]\mbox{}

\textbf{Primary Actor:} FPVR user

\textbf{Stakeholders of interest:} Novice FPV pilots, drone and gaming enthusiasts

\textbf{Precondition:} The application is running with all necessary hardware, a supported
headset and controller are available, and the main menu is visible from the VR.

\textbf{Main success scenario:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The pilot selects Free Flight from the main menu.
\item The system loads the free-flight environment and places the drone at its
starting position.
\item The application initializes the controller inputs and displays the flight view.
\item The pilot practises throttle, roll, pitch, and yaw in the open
environment.
\item The pilot takes off and manoeuvres the drone without course
restrictions.
\item The simulator provides no course timer, checkpoints, or score for the session.
\item If the drone crashes, the pilot opens the recovery option and resets the
drone.
\item The system returns the drone to its grounded starting position and restores
flight control.
\item The pilot continues practising and may repeat the crash-and-reset cycle.
\item The pilot selects Exit and the application returns to the main menu.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary scenarios:}
\begin{itemize}
\item If the pilot crashes repeatedly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The pilot selects Reset after each crash.
\item The system counts the contact as a crash and opens the recovery state.
\item The system clears the active motion and returns the drone to its grounded
starting position.
\item The pilot confirms that the controls are neutral and resumes practice.
\item The pilot repeats these steps for each subsequent crash; no course score
or completion result is created.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the controller disconnects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects the loss of the controller connection.
\item The system pauses flight input and prevents the last received axes from being
applied continuously.
\item The pilot reconnects or re-pairs the controller.
\item The application reports that the controller is available and asks the pilot to
place the controls in their neutral position.
\item The pilot applies neutral controls and explicitly confirms Resume.
\item The system restores flight control from the paused state.
\end{enumerate}
\item If an input axis is non-finite:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects a non-finite input value.
\item The system rejects the value instead of using it for flight or scoring.
\item The application pauses the session when possible and asks the user to release and
reapply the affected control.
\item The user restores valid controller input and explicitly resumes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\item[U-02: Running an Obstacle-Course Level]\mbox{}

\textbf{Primary Actor:} FPVR user

\textbf{Stakeholders of interest:} Novice FPV pilots, drone and gaming enthusiasts

\textbf{Precondition:} An obstacle-course level with ordered checkpoints and a
finish is available, and the user is at the level-selection interface.

\textbf{Main success scenario:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user opens the level-selection interface.
\item The application displays the available obstacle-course levels.
\item The user selects an available course.
\item The system loads the course and places the drone at the valid starting pose.
\item The system initializes the attempt, checkpoint progress, collision count, and
timer.
\item The user takes off and flies toward the first required checkpoint.
\item The user crosses each required checkpoint in the specified order.
\item The system records each valid checkpoint crossing and advances the required
checkpoint.
\item The user reaches the finish after all required checkpoints are complete.
\item The application validates the completed route, stops the timer, and opens the
current-attempt review.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary scenarios:}
\begin{itemize}
\item If the user crosses a later checkpoint before the required checkpoint:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system detects the checkpoint trigger.
\item The system compares the triggered checkpoint with the next required
checkpoint.
\item The system rejects the out-of-order crossing.
\item The application leaves the required checkpoint unchanged and informs the user to
return to the route.
\item The user flies through the required checkpoint before continuing toward
the later checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the user crosses an already-completed checkpoint:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system detects the repeated checkpoint trigger.
\item The system identifies the checkpoint as already recorded.
\item The system ignores the repeated trigger and preserves the existing progress.
\item The user continues from the current required checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the controller disconnects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects the loss of the controller connection.
\item The system pauses flight input and prevents the last received axes from being
applied continuously.
\item The pilot reconnects or re-pairs the controller.
\item The application reports that the controller is available and asks the pilot to
place the controls in their neutral position.
\item The pilot applies neutral controls and explicitly confirms Resume.
\item The system restores flight control from the paused state.
\end{enumerate}
\item If a required checkpoint is missing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects that the course does not contain the required checkpoint.
\item The system marks the attempt invalid and prevents a finish from becoming a
valid completion.
\item The system pauses the session when possible and informs the user of the
missing course data.
\item The user exits the invalid attempt or retries after the course data is
corrected.
\end{enumerate}
\item If an input axis is non-finite:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects a non-finite input value.
\item The system rejects the value instead of using it for flight or scoring.
\item The application pauses the session when possible and asks the user to release and
reapply the affected control.
\item The user restores valid controller input and explicitly resumes.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the drone position is out of bounds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system detects that the drone is outside the valid course
area.
\item FPVR informs the user that they are outside and must return to the main course
\item After a prolonged period, the system will reset the drone to the last valid position or checkpoint.
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\item[U-03: Recover from Crash during a Course.]\mbox{}

\textbf{Primary Actor:} FPVR user

\textbf{Stakeholders of interest:} FPV pilots

\textbf{Precondition:} The user is attempting an obstacle course and is
actively flying until they collide with an obstacle such as the ground, wall, or a goal.

\textbf{Main success scenario:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user flies an active obstacle-course attempt.
\item The drone collides with an obstacle, the ground, a wall, or a goal.
\item The system detects the collision and stops active flight input.
\item The system records one crash and displays the recovery menu.
\item The user selects Restart.
\item The application finds the most recently completed checkpoint, or the course start if
no checkpoint has been completed.
\item The system places the drone at the selected recovery position with a valid
orientation.
\item The system resets the section timer while preserving total time and crash
penalties.
\item The system restores the next required checkpoint and clears transient crash
state.
\item The user confirms neutral controls and resumes flight.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary scenarios:}
\begin{itemize}
\item If the drone makes repeated contact while the recovery menu is shown:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects the first collision and opens the recovery menu.
\item The system marks the crash as handled and disables additional crash
registration while the menu is displayed.
\item The system ignores subsequent contact events during that recovery state.
\item The user selects Restart or exits the attempt.
\item The system records no more than one crash for that collision event.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the user selects Restart as a new attempt:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The user chooses New Attempt rather than recovery at the checkpoint.
\item The application asks the user to confirm the restart.
\item The user confirms the restart.
\item The system creates a new attempt at the course start.
\item The system resets checkpoint progress, timers, collision count, and
performance data.
\item The user begins the new flight.
\end{enumerate}
\item If external networking or the optional AI service is unavailable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects the unavailable service or failed connection.
\item The system reports that the optional capability is unavailable.
\item The application disables only the dependent optional feature.
\item The user continues practising, recovering from crashes, and completing
the course using standard local behavior.
\item The system records and displays the standard result without waiting for the
unavailable service.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the user declines consent for optional AI analysis:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application requests consent before sending the required flight data.
\item The user selects Decline.
\item The system records that optional analysis is unavailable for this attempt and
does not send the data.
\item The application continues the standard flight, recovery, and review flow.
\item The system displays the standard result without changing its score.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the optional AI request times out:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application starts the optional request after consent is available.
\item The system reaches the configured timeout without a valid response.
\item The application cancels or abandons the optional request and reports the service as
unavailable.
\item The user continues to the standard result without waiting further.
\item The system preserves the recorded score and rating.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the optional AI output is malformed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application receives the optional response.
\item The system validates the response before displaying or applying it.
\item The application rejects malformed output and reports that AI feedback is
unavailable.
\item The system displays the standard review and preserves the recorded score,
rating, and other results.
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\item[U-04: Replay and Analyze Flight Performance]\mbox{}

\textbf{Primary Actor:} FPVR user

\textbf{Stakeholders of interest:} FPV pilots, drone training instructors,
and the team responsible for the optional AI feedback service.

\textbf{Precondition:} The user has completed at least one course and the
attempt has been recorded by the application. If AI feedback is enabled, the
user has approved the data required for the optional analysis.

\textbf{Main success scenario:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user opens the list of recorded flights.
\item The application displays the locally stored recordings and their available results.
\item The user selects a completed flight recording.
\item The system loads the stored virtual telemetry data and footage.
\item The application presents the recording in the VR or screen replay view.
\item The user plays the recording and may pause, rewind, or fast-forward it.
\item The system displays the standard performance results, including completion
time, checkpoint results, collision count, numerical score, and performance
rating.
\item If optional AI feedback is enabled and consent is available, the application sends
the permitted performance information for analysis.
\item The system displays the resulting observations within the review without
changing the standard results.
\item The user exits the replay and the application returns to the main menu.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary scenarios:}
\begin{itemize}
\item If the selected recording is corrupted:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application attempts to read and validate the recording metadata and content.
\item The system detects the corruption and stops loading the recording.
\item The application displays an error explaining that the recording cannot be replayed.
\item The user dismisses the error.
\item The application returns the user to the recording list or main menu without changing
the stored result.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the selected recording is missing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system looks up the selected recording and its referenced local files.
\item The application detects that one or more required files are absent.
\item The system displays an error and removes the unavailable item from the active
replay view.
\item The user dismisses the error and returns to the recording list or main
menu.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the recording is incompatible with the current application version:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application reads the recording version metadata.
\item The system compares it with the current supported recording format.
\item The application prevents incompatible data from being interpreted as a replay.
\item The system displays a warning and suggests updating the recording or the
application.
\item The user returns to the recording list or updates before trying again.
\end{enumerate}
\item If AI feedback is unavailable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application checks whether the optional service is reachable and enabled.
\item The system reports the unavailable capability.
\item The application skips the optional request.
\item The system displays the standard review and leaves the user in the replay
flow.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the user declines AI consent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application requests consent before sending permitted flight-performance data.
\item The user selects Decline.
\item The system does not send the data for optional analysis.
\item The application displays the standard review without optional observations.
\end{enumerate}
\item If AI feedback times out:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application starts the optional analysis request.
\item The system reaches the configured timeout without a usable response.
\item The application stops waiting and marks the analysis unavailable.
\item The system displays the standard review without interrupting replay controls.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the AI service fails to produce an analysis:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application receives an empty or failed analysis result.
\item The system reports that no optional feedback is available.
\item The application keeps the standard performance results visible.
\item The user continues or exits the replay normally.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the AI response is malformed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system validates the response before using it.
\item The application discards the malformed response.
\item The system records the optional analysis as unavailable.
\item The application displays the standard review without changing the recorded score,
rating, or other standard results.
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\item[U-05: Connecting to Multiplayer Training Session (optional)]\mbox{}

\textbf{Primary Actor:} FPVR user

\textbf{Stakeholders of interest:} FPV pilots

\textbf{Precondition:} The user is running the application, has a stable internet
connection, and has selected the optional multiplayer training mode. The application
should be able to connect to an external database or server.

\textbf{Main success scenario:}
\begin{itemize}
\item The user selects Multiplayer Training from the main menu.
\item The application verifies that the multiplayer option and network connection are
available.
\item The system requests a connection to an available training session.
\item The application joins the session and receives its participant and course data.
\item The system displays the participating users and selected training course.
\item The application initializes the user's local flight state and performance data.
\item The users complete the training session while the system synchronizes the
session state.
\item The system keeps each participant's flight state and performance data
separate.
\item The application detects the end of the session and validates the local result.
\item The system displays the session results and returns the user to the main menu.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Secondary scenarios:}
\begin{itemize}
\item If no training session is available:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application searches for an available training session.
\item The system finds no session that can accept the user.
\item The application informs the user that no training session is available.
\item The user selects Retry to search again or Exit to return to the main
menu.
\item The system performs the selected action without creating a partial
multiplayer result.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the connection is lost before the session starts:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects that the connection attempt failed.
\item The system cancels the incomplete join operation.
\item The application informs the user that the session could not be joined.
\item The user selects Retry or returns to the main menu.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the connection is lost during the session:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The system detects the lost connection.
\item The application pauses local multiplayer input and marks synchronization as
invalid.
\item The system attempts the supported reconnection behavior or waits for the
session to end.
\item If synchronization is restored, the application reports the restored state and the
user explicitly resumes.
\item If synchronization cannot be restored, the system ends the multiplayer
session, informs the user, and does not fabricate a result.
\item The user returns to the main menu or selects a single-user training
mode.
\end{enumerate}
\item If the multiplayer service is unavailable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The application detects that the service cannot be reached.
\item The system informs the user that Multiplayer Training is unavailable.
\item The application leaves the multiplayer attempt unstarted.
\item The user returns to the main menu and selects Free Flight or an
obstacle-course level.
\item The system starts the selected single-user training mode normally.
\end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}
\end{description}

\subsection{Verification and acceptance criteria}\label{s6}

This section states, for every requirement in S.2, how it will be
\textbf{verified} (confirming FPVR was built correctly against the
requirement) and how it will be \textbf{validated} (confirming the
requirement meets the real training need). Each requirement identifier
links to the S.2 subsection that defines it. Validation is by user
evaluation sessions, supervisor/owner review, or expert review.

\begingroup
\sloppy

\subsubsection{Drone Flight Simulation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-01}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Move and rotate the drone in a test scene and
 confirm the headset view matches drone position and orientation, with no
 offset or lag between them.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users report the view feels like sitting in a real FPV drone.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-02}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Apply each of throttle, pitch, roll, and yaw in
 isolation and in combination, and check that the drone's state responds in
 the correct axis and direction.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users with FPV experience confirm that the control
 response matches expectations.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-03}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Run the zero-thrust drop fixture in
 \hyperref[sec:proposed-performance]{PR-FLT-02} and compare the displacement with
 $gt^2/2$. Check that the drone falls when thrust is below hover level.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor review of whether the flight feel is adequate
 for training.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-04}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Fly into every obstacle type and ground surface in
 each environment and confirm contact is detected. Fly close to obstacles and
 confirm there are no false detections.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that crashes occur where they visually expect them.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-05}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Sit on the ground, then take off, and confirm no
 crash is registered. After takeoff, touch the ground and then an obstacle,
 and confirm a crash is registered each time.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that takeoff and landing feel natural, with
 no accidental crashes at takeoff.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-flight]{FR-FLT-06}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Trigger a crash and confirm exactly one crash event
 is recorded, flight input is blocked, and flight resumes only after a
 recovery action.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the crash response is clear and fair.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Training Mode and Level Management}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-01}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Enter free flight and confirm no checkpoints,
 timer, or score are shown or computed.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Novice users confirm free flight gives low-pressure practice.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-02}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Crash repeatedly in free flight and confirm reset
 restores a flyable drone each time.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm they can practise without frustration after a crash.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-03}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Count the selectable obstacle-course levels
 (at least \texttt{MIN\_LEVELS}) and inspect their configurations. Check that
 each loads and is completable.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users rank the levels by difficulty, and the ranking is
 compared with the intended difficulty.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-04}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Load each environment and confirm at least
 \texttt{MIN\_ENVIRONMENTS} distinct environments exist.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the environments look and feel different.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-05}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} On a fresh install, select every level in turn
 without completing any other level.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm they can pick any level freely.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-06}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Exit an active session to the main menu from free
 flight and from every level, and confirm the application does not restart or crash.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that exiting is easy to find and quick.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-levels]{FR-LVL-07}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Complete or abandon an attempt, start a new one, and
 inspect checkpoint progress, timers, collision count, and performance data
 for reset values.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor review confirms that attempts are independent.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Checkpoint and Crash Recovery}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-01}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Inspect each course definition for an ordered,
 non-empty checkpoint sequence.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the checkpoint order is understandable.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-02}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Fly through checkpoints in order and confirm
 progress advances by exactly one each time.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that checkpoint progress matches what they experience.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-03}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Fly through checkpoints out of order and confirm
 progress does not change.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the course cannot be skipped and that
 guidance makes the next checkpoint clear.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-04}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Crash during a course and confirm both the restart
 and respawn options are offered.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the options are clear.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-05}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Respawn after crashing before the first checkpoint,
 after checkpoint $n$, and at the last checkpoint. Check that the drone
 returns to the start or the last completed checkpoint.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that respawn lets them resume without confusion.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-06}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Respawn and confirm completed checkpoints, total
 time, and collision penalties are all preserved.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor review confirms respawn carries a fair penalty.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-07}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Restart the level and confirm checkpoint progress,
 total time, section time, collision count, and score are all reset.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that restart gives a clean attempt.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-checkpoints]{FR-CHK-08}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Reach the finish with all checkpoints completed in
 order, and again with a missed or out-of-order checkpoint. Only the first
 case may complete the level.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that completion feels earned and consistent.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Timing, Scoring, and Performance Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-01}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Run an attempt and compare the total timer with an
 independent stopwatch.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the timer is visible and trustworthy.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-02}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Crash during a section and confirm that the time
 spent in that section is included in the total.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor review confirms that failed sections should count.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-03}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Confirm the section timer starts from the beginning
 of each checkpoint segment and runs independently of the total timer.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users find per-section times useful for improving their flying.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-04}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Confirm the section timer resets on checkpoint
 completion and on respawn.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that the section timer reflects the current section.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-05}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Record the total time before and after respawn and
 confirm it does not drop.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor review confirms respawn keeps time consequences.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-06}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Open the crash-recovery menu, wait a known
 duration, and confirm total time stays frozen and resumes when flight continues.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm that menu time does not affect performance.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-07}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Cause a known number of crashes and confirm the
 collision count increases by one per crash.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm the collision count matches their experience.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-08}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Confirm the score matches the formula in
 \hyperref[sec:proposed-performance]{PR-SCORE-01} for given time and crash inputs,
 and that each added crash lowers the score.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Supervisor and user review of whether the score reflects
 flying quality.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-09}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Confirm the rating is computed from defined
 criteria separately from the score, including boundary cases.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} User evaluation of whether the rating labels are meaningful.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:s2-performance]{FR-PER-10}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Finish a course and confirm the displayed completion
 time equals the final total timer value and does not change afterwards.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users confirm the final time is trustworthy.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Proposed Quantitative and Interaction Specifications}
All of these remain PROPOSED. Their verification is defined now so the cases
are ready if they are approved.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:proposed-performance]{PR-PERF-01}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Run the \texttt{PERF\_RUN\_SECONDS} workload after
 \texttt{WARMUP\_SECONDS} of warm-up on every declared device, mode, and
 controller configuration and all released courses, including crashes, menus,
 and reviews. Check that the average frame rate is at least
 \texttt{MIN\_NATIVE\_FPS} and that \texttt{ON\_TIME\_FRAME\_FRACTION} of frame
 deadlines are met. Report synthetic frames separately.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users report comfort in sessions, and the threshold is
 reviewed by the supervisor. Platform release testing remains separate.
\item \textbf{\hyperref[sec:proposed-performance]{PR-PERF-02}}\par
 \textbf{Verification:} Measure the interval from application receipt of
 input to the visible flight response during the same workload. Check that the
 \texttt{LATENCY\_PERCENTILE} percentile does not exceed
 \texttt{INPUT\_RESPONSE\_MS}, and report maxima and invalid samples.\par
 \textbf{Validation:} Users report whether controls feel responsive. Hardware
 and PCVR-link validation follow.
